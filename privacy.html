Privacy Policy

Let's Appeal LLC · Last updated 1 August 2026

This explains what we collect, what we deliberately do not collect, and who else touches it. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

The short version. We ask for two things: your first name and your email address. The name so we can print it on your draft appeal letter, the email so we can send you a copy. We never ask for your denial letter, your claim number, your diagnosis, or anything else about your health or your insurance claim. We do not want that information and we do not store it.

Who we are

Let's Appeal LLC, based in Tampa, Florida. You can reach us any time at hello@letsappeal.com.

What we collect

  • Your first name and your email address, if you choose to enter them. Your First Letter, our free appeal tool, asks for these at the very end, after the questions and before it shows your results: the name is printed on your draft letter, and the email sends you a copy along with the free guide.
  • Standard website analytics: pages viewed, approximate location derived from your IP address, device and browser type, and how you arrived at the site. This is collected through cookies and similar technologies.
  • How the pages are used: how far down a page you scrolled, roughly how long you stayed, which links and buttons were clicked, and how far through the free tool's questions you got. This tells us which parts of the site are not working. It records the question number you reached, never the answer you gave.
  • Purchase details, if you buy the paid course. Payment is handled entirely by Whop. We never see or store your card number.

What we do not collect

This part matters more than the part above, so it gets its own section.

  • We do not ask you to upload, paste, or send us your denial letter.
  • We do not collect your claim number, member ID, policy number, diagnosis, treatment, medications, or the name of your insurer.
  • We do not ask for your surname, address, phone number, or date of birth.
  • Your First Letter runs entirely inside your own browser. Every answer you give it about your claim, the denial reason, the dates, your plan type, what your doctor said, is used to build your results on your own device and is never transmitted to us or to anyone else. Closing the tab erases it. Only the first name and email you type on the last screen are ever sent, and they are sent without any of the rest.
  • We never send health information to advertising platforms. See the advertising section below.

Why we collect it

  • To send you the free guide you asked for, and a short series of follow-up emails about the appeals process. Every one of those emails has an unsubscribe link, and it works immediately.
  • To understand which pages and which ads bring people to the site, so we do not waste money on ones that do not.
  • To deliver the paid course to you if you buy it.

We do not sell your email address. We do not rent it, trade it, or hand it to anyone building a marketing list.

Who processes your information

We use a small number of established services, and they only receive what they need to do their job:

  • Kit (formerly ConvertKit) stores your email address and sends our emails.
  • Whop handles course purchases and payment processing.
  • Vercel hosts the website.
  • Google Analytics and Google Ads provide website analytics and measure ad performance. If you buy the course, a scrambled one-way version of your email address is sent to Google Ads so it can tell which ad led to the sale. It cannot be turned back into your address, and it is used only for that matching.
  • Meta (Facebook and Instagram) measures ad performance through the Meta Pixel.
  • Microsoft Clarity records how pages are used, so we can see which parts of the site are confusing. See below for exactly what it does and does not capture.

Advertising and analytics, specifically

We advertise on Google and on Meta, and both use cookies to measure whether their ads work.

The Meta Pixel on this site sends six signals, and this is the whole list:

  • that a page was opened
  • that someone opened the free letter tool
  • that someone signed up for the guide
  • that someone finished a draft letter in the tool
  • that someone clicked through to buy the course
  • that someone completed a purchase, and the amount paid

The first five are a plain yes or no. The sixth also carries the amount paid, because that is the number that tells us whether an ad paid for itself. None of them carries your email address, your name, your answers, or anything at all about your health, your insurance plan, or your claim. Meta is told that something happened, never what you said or who you are.

The purchase signal reaches Meta from Whop rather than from your browser, because that is where the payment happens. To connect it to the right ad, we pass along the anonymous click identifier that Meta's own cookie already placed in your browser. We are handing Meta back its own identifier. We do not send your email address to make that match, even though that is the standard way of doing it and it would work better for us.

We have deliberately left off the Meta setting that would attach your email to those signals, because we do not think it is an appropriate trade in a category like this one. We do not send Meta how far you scrolled, how long you stayed, or what you clicked either, even though the option exists.

Session recordings, and what they hide

We use Microsoft Clarity to record how pages are used. A recording is a playback of mouse movement, scrolling, taps and clicks on the page. We watch these to find where the site confuses people. We are a very small operation, and this is how we find out that a page is not working without asking anyone to fill in a survey.

What the recordings deliberately do not show:

  • Anything you type. Your name, your email address, and any text you enter are masked before the recording ever leaves your browser. They are never transmitted and we cannot recover them.
  • Anything you answer in Your First Letter. The whole question area is masked. We can see that someone reached question six and stopped. We cannot see which denial reason they picked, what plan they have, or anything else about their claim.
  • Your draft letter. The results screen, including the letter itself, is masked in full.

Masking is enforced in two independent places: in our Clarity account settings, and written into the pages themselves, so the protection holds even if a setting is changed by accident.

Clarity does not receive your email address, and it is not an advertising platform. Microsoft's handling of this data is covered by the Microsoft Privacy Statement.

You can opt out of Google Analytics with the Google Analytics opt-out add-on, and you can control ad personalisation in your Meta ad preferences and Google Ad Settings. Most browsers also let you block or delete cookies.

How long we keep it

We keep your email address until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete it, whichever comes first. Analytics data is retained for 14 months and then deleted automatically.

Your choices

  • Unsubscribe using the link at the bottom of any email. It takes effect immediately.
  • Ask us to delete your information. Email hello@letsappeal.com and we will remove it and confirm when it is done.
  • Ask us what we hold about you. Same address. It will be a short answer, because it is an email address.

If you live somewhere with specific privacy rights, such as California, the EU, or the UK, you have those rights and the same address reaches us.

Children

This site is intended for adults. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18.

Security

The site is served over HTTPS, and the services listed above hold your email address under their own security programmes. No system is perfect, and we would rather say that plainly than promise otherwise.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we will update the date at the top and, for anything significant, say so by email.

One thing worth repeating, because it is the point of the business. Let's Appeal provides educational information only. It is not legal, medical, or insurance advice. Your own denial letter and your own plan documents are always the authority on your situation.
