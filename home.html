Questions?

Do I need a lawyer to appeal?

Most internal appeals are written by the patient or their doctor's office rather than a lawyer. The Playbook is built for doing it yourself. It is educational material, not legal representation.

Is this legal or medical advice?

No. Let's Appeal is educational. We show you how the appeals process works and give you the templates and scripts. Every plan is different, and the deadline printed on your own denial letter is always the authority.

Will this work for my type of plan?

The Playbook covers Marketplace, Medicare, Medicaid and employer/ERISA plans separately, because the rules and the deadlines differ. Module 2 helps you work out which one you are on, and Your First Letter tells you for free in about three minutes.

What if my deadline has already passed?

Check your letter before you assume it has. Deadlines vary by plan type, and the date that matters is the one printed on your notice. Your First Letter takes that date and works out the day your appeal is actually due.

Is Your First Letter really free?

Yes. Ten questions, no account and no card, and nothing to upload. You get your deadline, which rules govern your plan, and a draft appeal letter. We ask for your first name and email at the end, the name because it is printed on the letter.

Do I have to send you my denial letter?

No, and we will not accept it. You read your own letter and answer questions about it. Nothing you type into the tool is transmitted to us or stored anywhere, and closing the tab erases it. That is written into our privacy policy.
